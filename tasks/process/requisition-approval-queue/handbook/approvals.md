# Approving requisitions (APR)

**APR-1.1** Approval limits for a requisition's total:

| Approver | Limit |
|---|---|
| Department head | $2,500 |
| Purchasing manager | $10,000 |
| Controller | $50,000 |
| President | above $50,000 |

Individual limits are on each user's record in the ERP.

**APR-1.2** Never approve a requisition you raised. Forward it to your own manager.

**APR-2.1** Approve only requisitions within your limit. Forward anything larger to the approver with the lowest
limit that covers it.

**APR-2.2** Requisitions from the same requester for the same vendor, raised within five business days of each
other, count as one requisition for approval limits. When their combined total is above your limit, forward every
one of them to the approver with the lowest limit that covers the combined total. Never approve part of such a
group.

**APR-3.1** Before approving a requisition charged to an expense account, check the department's remaining budget
for that account for the fiscal year to date, through the current month: budget, less actual spending, less
commitments (open purchase orders and approved requisitions not yet ordered). The budget-vs-actual report shows
all three. If the requisition is larger than what remains, do not approve it: forward it to the controller with
the reason budget.

**APR-3.2** Capital purchases (charged to 1500 Machinery and equipment) are approved by amount only; APR-3.1 does
not apply to them.

**APR-4.1** Return, rather than reject, a requisition that names a vendor that is inactive or on quality hold, so
the requester can choose an approved vendor. Say why in the reason.

**APR-4.2** A requisition that repeats another open requisition from the same requester, with the same lines, is a
duplicate. Reject the later one with the reason duplicate, and decide the earlier one on its merits.

**APR-5.1** Decide every requisition waiting for you on the day it is in your queue.
